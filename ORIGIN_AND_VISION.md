# Origin and Vision

> Also available in [中文](ORIGIN_AND_VISION.zh.md). Both versions were
> written directly by the author — neither is a translation of the
> other.

GPT-6 Astra is out, and once again everyone is talking about how much
stronger models are getting, how close AGI might be, and what the next
generation will be able to do.

That's great. AI should get stronger. Faster. Smarter. No argument
there.

But here's what I keep wondering:

If Olympic athletes exist, does that mean everyone else should stop
exercising?

Everybody wants the Ivy League grad, the MIT genius, the superstar
hire.

Fine.

But what, the community-college guy doesn't deserve a job?

Work is not just an intelligence contest. A lot of it is management.

Manage an average employee well, and they can do excellent work.
Manage a brilliant employee badly, and they can still create a mess.

AI is no different.

Right now, whenever AI screws something up, the answer often seems to
be:

The model isn't good enough. Wait for the next one.

Then the next one comes.

Something still goes wrong.

So we wait again.

At some point I have to ask: how long are we planning to wait? Until
AGI descends from the heavens and fixes everything for us?

I'm not waiting for the Messiah model.

I have work to do today.

That is roughly where PRAOP came from.

The basic idea is simple:

AI is a probabilistic intelligent agent.

But so are we.

You expect AI to never make a mistake?

Can you promise that you never will?

You expect AI to always be reliable?

Are humans always reliable?

The difference is that we humans have spent our entire lives learning
how other humans fail.

People forget things. Misunderstand instructions. Get stubborn. Make
assumptions. Become overconfident. Go down the wrong path and keep
going.

None of this surprises us very much anymore.

We have managers, reviews, handoffs, checklists, training, escalation
paths, institutional memory — all kinds of mechanisms built around the
simple fact that human beings are imperfect.

Now imagine you just hired a new coworker.

Except this coworker is basically an alien.

Brilliant. Fast. Obedient.

And occasionally unreliable in ways you would never have thought to
anticipate.

So what do you do?

You observe it. You quietly keep track of how it behaves. You learn
its patterns. You adjust how you work together.

You try something.

It breaks.

You write it down.

You change the way you work.

Then you try again.

This is what PRAOP actually is.

I don't want to treat AI as just another tool.

I also don't want to pretend it is a human.

I think it is more useful to treat it as a new kind of intelligent
agent whose behavior we are still learning to understand.

Observe it.

Record what happens.

Look for patterns.

Develop ways of working with it.

Try them.

Revise them.

Try again.

That is also why PRAOP should never become a fixed rulebook or some
grand theory claiming to be universally true.

It should be a living methodology.

A growing collection of real cases.

A growing body of patterns distilled from those cases.

A growing set of practices that people actually try in the real world.

And then, when reality proves us wrong, we change them.

Anyone who seriously uses PRAOP is not just a user of PRAOP.

They are also an observer, a researcher, and a practitioner.

That, I think, is where PRAOP gets its vitality.
